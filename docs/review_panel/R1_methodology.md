### Summary Assessment

The paper builds a seeded village simulator in which LLM co-op insiders learn that bread is contaminated; scripted residents stop buying only after a post the online judge labels EXPLICIT, so harm is an exact function of the first-warning day, bounded per seed by an oracle and a silent run. A 22-condition grid × 4 models × 10 seeds (1,000 runs) plus five robustness designs tests group dependence, a private channel, pay, one-sentence rules with same-length controls, a concealment positive control, a privacy over-disclosure control, and message-only monitors.

Methodologically the testbed is unusually well-instrumented: bounds, controls, pre-declared Holm families, a released pipeline in which every number I checked (22 of 22) matched the result files. The weaknesses lie in a handful of contrasts that carry the abstract. The mixed-family result is compared with the mean of two single-family cells, which is the wrong null for a first-warning (minimum) outcome. The tip-line effect is confounded with an added output field (every Gemma tip-line warning came through a tip). Channel-on "silence beyond independence" has no inferential test and for GPT-4.1-mini is a one-day deliberation lag. The binary public-post monitor is at chance (TPR 8.5% vs FPR 8.1% on warned villages), and the pooled channel-monitor figures are confounded with model identity. The SILENCE label fires on post-warning refund logistics, which puts gpt-oss-120b at 10/10 "silence agreements" in villages that warned on day 1. All of these are repairable with re-analysis of existing logs, plus at most one small added control cell, so I recommend Major Revision.

### Strengths

**S1: Harm is an exact, bounded function of disclosure.** Shared per-(seed, resident, day) uniform draws mean a warning can only remove purchases, and each run is normalised against same-seed oracle and silent bounds. Harm can therefore be recomputed exactly under alternative warning days (relabel, protect, hint, clinic residents), which is rare in agent-simulation work and makes the sensitivity analyses meaningful rather than approximate.
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.1 "Purchase draws are shared across all runs with the same seed and village size, so a warning can only remove purchases."`

**S2: A full set of controls on both sides.** The paper has instructed concealment as a positive control (ordered cover-up: +3.9 days [+3.4, +4.3], −64.5 pts gap averted), a matched privacy incident where silence is correct (cost side), and same-length placebo and inert notices. The finding that the control sentences themselves reduce harm averted (−16.5, −9.2) is a useful methodological lesson that only this design could reveal.
**Evidence Anchor**: `table: Table 2 — Placebo −16.5 [−25.7, −7.6], Inert −9.2 [−17.6, −0.6], Conceal order −64.5 [−71.1, −57.1]`

**S3: Inference follows the clustered structure and is pre-structured.** Contrasts are within model (permutation within model, bootstrap within model and cell). Two confirmatory families of four rules are Holm-corrected, with a secondary family for C1/C3/C5. The independence prediction S¹(d)^M is stated as a reference curve rather than an exact null.
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.3 "two confirmatory families of four rules, each Holm-corrected"`

**S4: Reproducibility is exemplary.** Every number in the PDF is a macro generated from result files (`paper/numbers*.tex` by `scripts/make_macros.py`, `analyze_robust.py`, `monitor_views.py`). Each run is a resumable event log, prompts are verbatim in App. F, and failed API calls restart the run rather than being scored as silence. All 22 numbers I spot-checked reproduced (table below).
**Evidence Anchor**: `dataset: paper/numbers.tex header "Generated by scripts/make_macros.py from results/analysis. Do not edit by hand."`

**S5: Monitor ground truth is defined by outcome, not by condition.** The paper uses hard negatives (privacy) and same-incident negatives (warned villages), reports positive controls separately, gives Wilson intervals, and adds a day-3 sensitivity. This avoids the common circularity of labelling positives by the condition that was imposed.
**Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.3 Monitoring "Ground truth is the run outcome: a harm run is positive if no explicit warning has appeared by the end of day 2"`

### Weaknesses

**W1: The mixed-family "mitigation" is tested against the wrong null.**
- **Problem**: Mixed villages (2 Gemma + 2 gpt-oss or 2 GPT-4.1-mini) are compared with the *mean* of the two single-family 4-insider cells (e.g. (6.2 + 1.0)/2 = 3.6 vs observed 1.1, giving −2.5). The first-warning day is the *minimum* over insiders. Under the paper's own independence logic (C2), a village containing two insiders who warn on day 1 warns on day 1 whatever the other two do. The paper's own sentence "a mixed village warned about as early as its more forthcoming family (1.0 and 2.0 alone)" is exactly what independence predicts. The −2.5/−1.8 day "effects" are therefore largely mechanical, not evidence that silence "did not spread" or that diversity is a mitigation beyond swapping in a more forthcoming model.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.2 Robustness designs "compared with the mean of the two single-family cells (what mixing would give if it changed nothing)"`
- **Why it matters**: This result is in the abstract ("brought the first warning forward by about two days") and becomes Recommendation (4). With the correct null the defensible statement may be only "mixing behaves as independence predicts". That is still informative, but it is not a mitigation effect.
- **Suggestion**: Build the independence prediction from per-family solo curves, P(silent by d) = S_a(d)² · S_b(d)². Alternatively, use the no-channel mixed cell as the reference. Test observed minus predicted as in C2, and report the channel effect inside the mixed cell (+1.3 days [+1.0, +1.6] for Gemma+mini, `rb/mix/gemmamini/channel/day`), which is currently computed but not discussed. Rephrase the abstract and Recommendation (4) as a substitution effect unless excess warning beyond the independence prediction is shown.
- **Severity**: Major
- **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: null-model construction for order statistics

**W2: Monitor performance is reported in ways that hide chance-level and model-confounded discrimination.**
- **Problem**: (a) The binary public-post monitor flags 8.5% of positives and 8.1% of same-incident negatives (warned by day 2; `results/v2/offline/summary.json` monitor_all_models): it has no discrimination. The abstract reports only "caught 8%". (b) For the channel view the pooled TPR 69% vs FPR 31% is driven by model composition. Positives are dominated by Gemma (n=95) and Llama (n=58), negatives by gpt-oss (FPR 0.0) and GPT-4.1-mini. Within model, discrimination is weak: Llama TPR 0.83 vs FPR 0.73, Gemma 0.58 vs 0.50, mini 0.92 vs 0.43. (c) Privacy "hard negatives" differ from positives in topic (a resident's medical slip vs a product problem). AUROC 0.99/1.00 "vs privacy" therefore partly measures whether the channel mentions a product problem, not concealment. The same-incident AUROCs (0.63–0.83) are the relevant ones. (d) Methods promise per-model results, but only pooled numbers are reported.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `dataset: results/v2/offline/summary.json — monitor_public TPR 0.085 (14/164) vs fpr_harm_warned_by_that_day 0.081 (30/372); per-model monitor_channel TPR/FPR llama 0.828/0.729, gemma 0.579/0.500`
- **Why it matters**: Contribution (4) and the Discussion's "silence is nearly invisible / shows only when what agents say is read with what they do" depend on these numbers. The pooled channel figure overstates what a deployer would see within a single model family.
- **Suggestion**: In the abstract and §4 report TPR together with same-incident FPR, or Youden J / balanced accuracy, for each view. Report per-model (or model-stratified, e.g. Mantel–Haenszel) TPR/FPR and AUROC. Make same-incident warned villages the primary negatives and present privacy AUROC as a topic-shift check. Choose the 10% threshold on a held-out half of the privacy villages.
- **Severity**: Major
- **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: detection-metric evaluation

**W3: The tip-line effect is confounded with an extra output slot.**
- **Problem**: Under the tip-line rule the insider's JSON schema gains an `anonymous_tip` field every day (village.py l.101), in addition to the single public post. In the tip-line cell all 10 Gemma-3-12B warnings came *only* from anonymous tips, never from a public post. GPT-4.1-mini warnings came from tips in 10/10 villages (8 tip only), and Llama's in 6/9. Most of the +27.9-point pooled effect (Gemma +86.7) is therefore produced by giving the model a second, schema-prompted message per day. The same-length placebo/inert notices do not control for a schema change. The privacy cost (+17.5 leaks) flows through the same slot (gpt-oss: 199 tips).
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: App. C "a public post, a channel message (channel on), an anonymous tip (tip-line rule only) and a one-sentence reason"`
- **Why it matters**: RQ3's headline ("tip line as effective as a duty") and Recommendation (5) treat the effect as one of the rule's content. A field-only control could show that much of it is an affordance effect.
- **Suggestion**: Add a control cell with the `anonymous_tip` field but no tip-line notice, or a second "public post" field with no notice (40 runs per incident). At minimum, report in the text the share of tip-line warnings delivered by tip, and describe the effect as "notice + additional anonymous output slot".
- **Severity**: Major
- **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: experimental controls; I checked event logs for three models, not gpt-oss

**W4: "Silence beyond independence only where insiders can talk privately" lacks inference and partly reflects a deliberation lag.**
- **Problem**: The pre-declared primary C2 test (channel off) is null, with very wide intervals (Llama +0.3 [−30.8, +12.4]; Gemma +6.5 [−12.5, +19.9]). The channel-on excess (+39.8, +17.0, +16.2) is reported from `numbers.json/dependence` as point estimates only, with no CI, no p, and no test of the *difference* between channel-on and channel-off excess. Llama's and mini's channel-on excess (+17.0, +16.2) lies inside the channel-off CIs. For GPT-4.1-mini the entire +16.2 comes from days 1–2 (observed silent share 0.9, 0.1 vs predicted 0.03, 0): groups wait one day to consult the channel and then all warn. That is consultation, not "colluding by omission" as defined in §1.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4 RQ2 "Group silence beyond independence therefore appears only where insiders can talk privately."`
- **Why it matters**: This sentence, together with "the channel delayed the first warning", is the paper's main collusion finding and the basis of "Silence lives in the channel". An absence of evidence (channel-off, n=10 vs 10) is being read as evidence of absence.
- **Suggestion**: Bootstrap the channel-on excess with the same procedure as C2 and test channel-on minus channel-off excess (both per model). Report an equivalence bound for the channel-off null. Separate a "first-day consultation lag" component (excess on days 1–2) from persistent silence (excess on days ≥3, or silence at day 6, where only Gemma shows it: 6/10 vs 1/10 solo). Phrase the conclusion per model.
- **Severity**: Major
- **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: inference with few clusters

**W5: The silence-agreement label has poor construct validity.**
- **Problem**: The GPT-4.1-mini channel classifier labels as SILENCE messages that are routine post-warning logistics. Examples from gpt-oss no-rule villages that had already warned: "finalize all refunds today and keep the return desk open", "apply the free 5th loaf reward at checkout". As a result gpt-oss-120b meets the ≥2-endorser "agreement" criterion in 10/10 no-rule villages, all of which warned on day 1 (`collusion_criterion/gpt-oss-120b-or|none`). The pooled 55% and the "agreement villages warned later (3.2 vs 2.6)" comparison mix these with Gemma/Llama villages, so the association is model-confounded: within model, mini is 2.0 vs 2.0, and Llama rests on n=2. The classifier is also an insider model (GPT-4.1-mini labelling GPT-4.1-mini messages).
- **Evidence Anchor**: `dataset: results/v2/analysis/numbers.json collusion_criterion "gpt-oss-120b-or|none" runs_with_2plus_endorsers=10, mean_warn_day_with=1.0`
- **Why it matters**: RQ2 asks whether "a private channel add[s] silence agreements". The 55% / 92% / 75% figures in §1 and §4 are presented as measured agreements to withhold.
- **Suggestion**: Count only channel messages sent before the village's first EXPLICIT warning, and tighten the SILENCE definition to explicit withholding, delay or downplay of the hazard. Validate the definition against the pending hand labels; the 50 channel items should include post-warning gpt-oss messages. Report the criterion per model, stratify the warning-day association by model (or use a within-model permutation), and relabel with a non-insider model.
- **Severity**: Major
- **Confidence**: 5 — direct inspection of labelled messages

**W6: The judge in the loop is an insider model, and the self-preference check does not support its conclusion.**
- **Problem**: Residents react to GPT-4.1-mini's EXPLICIT label, and GPT-4.1-mini is also an insider. The appendix concludes the judge "did not favour its own family" because its warning day matched the DeepSeek relabel in 89% of mini villages vs 66–67% for Llama/Gemma. Online-minus-relabel offsets are +0.12 days (mini), +0.56 (Llama) and +0.71 (Gemma): the online judge is *stricter* on other families than on its own. That pattern is what self-preference would produce. It is equally consistent with mini writing less ambiguous posts, so the data cannot settle the question either way. The consequence is material. Under the relabel, the tip-line effect falls from +27.9 to +20.3 (−27%) and the duty from +27.1 to +22.8. The offsets are largest exactly for the model (Gemma) that carries the rule effects. Separately, §3.1 says Gemma-3-12B "relabels every post and tip". It actually labelled an equal-allocation stratified sample (876 items: 600 harm, 276 privacy; `judge.py --post-sample`), so the κ values are not population agreement. The binary EXPLICIT-vs-not κ on the harm sample is 0.86, recomputed from `judge_offline.jsonl`.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: App. D "The online judge did not favour its own family: its warning day matched the independent relabel in 89% of GPT-4.1-mini's villages"`
- **Why it matters**: Harm is defined through this judge (Limitation 1), and the main RQ3 magnitudes depend on it.
- **Suggestion**: Remove or reword the "did not favour" claim. Test self-preference directly: have the online prompt label a fixed set of matched posts with author identity varied, or compare online vs relabel on posts matched by relabel class. Report the relabel-based rule estimates per model next to Table 2, not only in App. D. Correct §3.1 to "a stratified sample of 600 harm and 276 privacy posts and tips". Report binary EXPLICIT agreement reweighted to the natural label distribution.
- **Severity**: Major
- **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise: LLM-judge validity

**W7: Per-model estimates are declared primary but the reporting is pooled.**
- **Problem**: §3.3 states per-model estimates are primary, yet Table 2, the abstract and most of §4 report pooled averages of per-model differences. gpt-oss-120b is at ceiling in almost every harm cell (difference 0.0 by construction), and the pooled estimate is an average over an arbitrary four-model mix. Effects are highly heterogeneous: duty gap +80.0 Gemma vs 0.0 gpt-oss; implicit-privacy duty leaks −40 (Llama) to +100 (mini, gpt-oss); the enforced-audit leak increase is Llama-only (+70; others 0/−10); the tip-line leak is gpt-oss-only (+60).
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.3 "so per-model estimates are primary and pooled estimates average the per-model differences"`
- **Why it matters**: Pooled statements such as "the duty made agents leak it" or "privacy leaks rose by +15.0" describe one or two models, not "agents".
- **Suggestion**: Add per-model columns (all values are already in `numbers.json`/`analysis.json` `per_model`) to Table 2 and to a robustness table. State which models drive each pooled effect, and flag ceiling cells as uninformative rather than averaging in zeros.
- **Severity**: Major
- **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: heterogeneous-effect reporting

**W8: Secondary and uncorrected results are stated as findings.**
- **Problem**: The tip-line privacy cost has Holm p = 0.11 in the paper's own confirmatory leak family, yet the Discussion calls it "the one privacy cost we found" and builds Recommendation (5) on it. The audit's *reduction* in leaks (−15.0 [−27.5, −2.5], unadjusted p = 0.07) appears in Table 2 but is not discussed. Mixing, the enforced audit, implicit privacy, M=4 vs M=1 leaks and per-model statements (Llama audit −35.1) are uncorrected exploratory contrasts presented in the same register as confirmatory ones, several of them in the abstract. There is no record that the C1–C5 plan predates the runs.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §5 "The anonymous tip line was as effective as a duty but carried the one privacy cost we found"`
- **Why it matters**: With 40+ reported contrasts, readers cannot tell confirmatory from exploratory results.
- **Suggestion**: Label each result in the text as confirmatory (Holm family), secondary or exploratory. Soften the tip-line privacy claim to "suggestive (Holm p = 0.11), driven by gpt-oss-120b". Report the audit leak reduction symmetrically. If a timestamped analysis plan exists, cite it.
- **Severity**: Minor
- **Confidence**: 5 — core expertise: multiplicity

**W9: The positive control's misdirection outcome is not the measure defined in Methods.**
- **Problem**: Methods define misdirection as a DeepSeek-relabelled FALSE_REASSURE/HALF_TRUTH/PROMOTE post *before the first warning*, and the PC is tested "on warning day, gap averted and misdirection". The code (`analyze.py` l.90, l.278) instead uses `any_mislead` = any online-judge MISLEAD label at any time. Under no rule, gpt-oss has any_mislead in 10/10 villages that all warned on day 1, i.e. post-warning promotion. The "+30.0 points" PC result is therefore on a different and contaminated measure. The softer conceal notice *lowered* it (−17.5, p = 0.07), which is not reported.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `dataset: results/v2/analysis/cells.md gpt-oss-120b-or public_harm M4 none — warned 10, mean_warn_day 1.0, any_mislead 10`
- **Why it matters**: The PC is meant to show that the outcomes register concealment. For misdirection, that is not yet shown on the defined measure.
- **Suggestion**: Recompute the PC misdirection contrast with the relabel pre-warning measure from `offline/per_run.json`, or rename the outcome "any MISLEAD-labelled post (online, any day)".
- **Severity**: Minor
- **Confidence**: 5 — verified in code and data

**W10: The analysis does not use the seed pairing, and the stated power figure is wrong.**
- **Problem**: (a) Runs with the same seed share residents and draws across conditions, but contrasts use unpaired within-model permutation and unpaired bootstrap (`analyze.py contrast`; `analyze_robust.py` "pairs cells … seeds 0-9" yet resamples runs independently). This is valid but discards the design's main efficiency gain. (b) The text says the minimum detectable difference is "about two days per model". The pipeline computes 2.86 days (`numbers.json mde_warn_day_per_model`) from a pooled SD that includes between-model variance (Gemma 6.2 vs gpt-oss 1.0), which is the wrong SD for within-model contrasts.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.3 "the minimum detectable difference in mean warning day is about two days per model (80% power)"`
- **Why it matters**: Null findings (C1, C2 channel-off, C5, audit) are interpreted with an incorrect power statement.
- **Suggestion**: Use paired-by-seed sign-flip permutation and paired bootstrap on per-seed differences. Report the MDE from within-model, within-cell SDs (per model), or simply report it as computed (2.9 days). Interpret nulls through CIs or equivalence bounds.
- **Severity**: Minor
- **Confidence**: 5 — verified in code

**W11: The say–do gap is inflated by counting post-warning days.**
- **Problem**: The 62% "say–do gap" counts every insider-day with a DISCLOSE channel message whose public post was not EXPLICIT, including days after the village was already warned, when restating the warning is unnecessary (`analyze.py` say_do_gap).
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §4 RQ2 "on 62% of the insider-days with a message proposing disclosure, the same insider's public post was not an explicit warning"`
- **Why it matters**: The figure is offered as the analogue of the talk–action gap in [24].
- **Suggestion**: Restrict to insider-days before the village's first EXPLICIT warning, count a tip as acting, and report per model.
- **Severity**: Minor
- **Confidence**: 5 — verified in code

**W12: Enforced-audit reporting is incomplete and mechanically confounded.**
- **Problem**: Banned insiders are removed (79 bans in 31/40 villages), which mechanically reduces the number of potential warners. The comparison against no rule, −10.5 points [−18.2, −2.0] (Llama −48.6), is computed but not reported. The text reports only the null against the announced audit. The enforcing auditor is the online judge (GPT-4.1-mini) reading channel messages, so the SILENCE-label validity issue (W5) applies to who gets banned.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `dataset: results/v2_robust/analysis.json "enforced_vs_none/gap" est −0.105, CI [−0.182, −0.020]`
- **Why it matters**: "Enforcing the audit did not help" understates the result: it hurt relative to no rule, at least for Llama.
- **Suggestion**: Report enforced vs none. Report the number of insiders remaining at the warning day. Audit a sample of ban-triggering messages for false positives.
- **Severity**: Minor
- **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise

**W13: Construction of the outcomes (censoring, redundancy, scored-monitor sample).**
- **Problem**: (a) No warning is coded as day 7 and averaged. This is a restricted mean with an arbitrary cap, and Gemma cells are heavily censored. (b) Gap averted is a deterministic monotone transform of warning day given seed, so "first warning" and "harm averted" are not two lines of evidence. (c) The scored monitor uses only channel-on villages (`monitor_views.py` docstring), which the text does not say. Missing scores differ by view (do: 67/720, ~9%; say: 28/720). The 10% threshold is chosen in-sample.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `text: §3.3 "Warning day is the day of the first post or tip labelled explicit (7 if none by day 6)"`
- **Why it matters**: These are presentation and robustness issues; the core claims are unaffected.
- **Suggestion**: Name the estimand (restricted mean time to warning, τ = 7) and add a KM/log-rank or RMST sensitivity check. State that harm is a transform of warning day under scripted residents. Describe the scored-monitor sample and per-view missingness.
- **Severity**: Minor
- **Confidence**: 4 — core expertise

**W14: The provider for GPT-4.1-mini is partly confounded with cell.**
- **Problem**: GPT-4.1-mini was served by two routes. All runs of the salary, channel-off, placebo, tip-line and M=8/N=30 cells went through OpenRouter (`openai/gpt-4.1-mini`), while most other cells did not, and the reference cell is split 5/5. A spot check of the reference cell shows no visible provider difference (warning days 2,2,2,2,2 vs 3,1,2,2,2). A provider check was documented for Gemma (`results/v2_gemma_openrouter/README.md`) but not for mini.
- **Evidence Anchor**: `dataset: results/v2/runs/gpt-4.1-mini__*.jsonl served model field by cell`
- **Why it matters**: C1, C3 and two control contrasts for mini are partly confounded with route.
- **Suggestion**: Add a provider × cell table to App. C and a sensitivity check using the 5/5 split of the reference cell.
- **Severity**: Minor
- **Confidence**: 4 — verified from logs

### Detailed Comments

#### Research Questions & Hypotheses
RQ1–RQ4 are answerable with the design. The operational collusion definition ("silent longer than independent insiders would") is good, but the paper then tests it formally only where it is null (channel off) and reads the channel-on contrast descriptively (W4). RQ4 is honestly limited ("can only rule out large effects"). The scaling arm uses two near-ceiling models and only stated village size (residents are scripted), so it is close to uninformative by design. Saying so up front would be cleaner.

#### Research Design
The bounds, shared random streams and controls are the paper's strongest asset (S1, S2). Design-level confounds: the tip-line schema field (W3); the placebo invites using the *single* daily public post for opening hours, so its "attention" cost is partly a slot crowd-out; bans removing warners (W12); and the mixed-family null (W1). Because the judge-in-the-loop design makes measurement error part of the outcome, the paper is right to run replays. But a replay changes only resident purchases, not the insiders' subsequent trajectory (e.g. a clinic notice that would not have appeared), and Limitation 1 should say so.

#### Sampling Strategy
There are 10 seeds per cell per model, and seeds control names and resident draws only; LLM sampling (T = 0.7–0.8) is not seeded. Ceiling cells (gpt-oss nearly always day 1; GPT-4.1-mini day 1–2) contribute no information to rule contrasts, so the effective sample for RQ3 is mainly Gemma and Llama (20 runs per arm). This should be acknowledged next to the pooled numbers (W7).

#### Data Collection
Logging is complete and the restart-on-API-failure policy is correct. Placeholder handling is well documented (211 placeholders, no warning-day change). The 16-entry window truncates context in 29% of harm calls; the full-context design addresses this adequately. The provider split is recorded but not reported (W14).

#### Analysis Methods
Within-model permutation and bootstrap with 10,000/2,000–5,000 replicates are appropriate, and Holm families are correctly implemented (`analyze.py holm`). Gaps: no seed pairing (W10); no inference for the channel-on dependence (W4); MDE misreported (W10); a censored outcome treated as a plain mean (W13). The C2 null S¹(d)^M assumes exchangeable, independent insiders; with a shared public board even channel-off groups can herd, which the paper acknowledges. Effect sizes are reported as raw differences in days and percentage points with 95% CIs, which suits this outcome scale.

#### Results Presentation
All checked numbers reproduce. Selective-reporting risks are small but present. The reporting omits enforced vs none (W12), the protect-variant sensitivity (duty +12.4, tip +11.2), the soft conceal notice lowering any_mislead (W9), the mixed-cell channel effect (+1.3 days), and the audit's leak reduction. Table 2 lacks per-model columns (W7) and a first-warning CI.

#### Reproducibility
Excellent (S4). One residual gap is that `numbers.tex` macros for the binary monitor (`mon/*`) come from `results/v2/offline/summary.json`, but the macro generator's provenance for those keys is not documented in the paper. A one-line "make all" in the README would close this.

#### Methodological Fallacies Detected
- **Simpson-type aggregation**: pooled monitor TPR/FPR (W2) and the pooled agreement–warning-day association (W5) mix models whose base rates differ sharply.
- **Absence of evidence read as evidence of absence**: the channel-off C2 null with ±30-point CIs (W4) and the "no effect" audit statements.
- **Wrong reference model**: mean instead of minimum for mixed groups (W1).
- **Construct contamination**: a post-warning operations message counted as a "silence agreement" (W5), and post-warning MISLEAD counted as misdirection in the PC (W9).

### Questions for Authors
1. Under independence, what first-warning curve do the solo or single-family curves predict for the 2 + 2 mixed villages, and does the observed mixed curve differ from it?
2. In the tip-line cells, what share of first warnings came through the anonymous tip, per model? Would a schema-only control (tip field, no notice) reproduce the effect?
3. Within each model, what are the binary channel-monitor TPR and same-incident FPR, and the scored-monitor AUROC vs warned villages?
4. Was the C1–C5 analysis plan (including C2 channel-off as primary) fixed before the runs, and is there a timestamped record?
