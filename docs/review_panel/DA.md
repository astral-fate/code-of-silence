### Acknowledged Strengths

The design has features most sprint reports lack: oracle and silent harm bounds per seed, an instructed-concealment positive control that moves every outcome as expected (+3.9 days, -64.5 points), a matched privacy incident that prices over-disclosure, same-length control notices, a monitor fixed before outcomes were inspected, and resident replays under three alternative behaviour models. The challenges below concern interpretation, not data hygiene.

### Strongest Counter-Argument

The evidence is equally well explained without any collusion. Turning the channel on does two things at once: it gives agents a private place to talk, and it adds a line to the system prompt and a second output field to every daily reply. The authors' own controls show that adding one irrelevant sentence delays the first warning by +1.0 day (placebo), the same size as the channel effect (-1.0 day when removed), and with the same per-model pattern: zero for gpt-oss-120b, largest for the weaker instruction-followers. The channel content does not look like a cartel either: 55% of 4,034 channel messages propose telling residents and only 23% propose silence, while on 62% of insider-days with a disclose message the same insider posted no public warning. That is the signature of substitution, agents writing their warning in the place they were just handed, not of agreement. The motive that collusion needs does nothing: salary versus sales moves warning day by +0.1. The silence is concentrated in one model, Gemma-3-12B, whose "silence" is largely hedged hints that a GPT-4.1-mini judge declines to call explicit (the two judges agree on its warning day in 67% of villages), and whose protective posts remove 57% of its harm once counted. On this reading, the duty and tip line "work" because they tell one hesitant model what to write; the audit "fails" because it gives no instruction; mixed families "break the silence" because first-warning day is a minimum over insiders; and the say+do monitor reaches AUROC 0.99 because its negatives are a different incident. What remains is a well-instrumented finding that one mid-size model hedges about contamination and that a one-sentence instruction fixes it. That is useful, but it is not evidence that LLM agents collude by omission.

### Issue List

The issues below are prose-introduced here; the band tables follow.

Summary of the single CRITICAL: the causal attribution of silence to a private coordination channel is confounded with prompt and output load, and the paper's own placebo evidence shows an effect of identical magnitude from load alone. This is fixable by one additional condition (a channel-on cell in which the extra field is not shared, or a shared field that is public), but until then the "silence lives in the channel" and "collude by omission" readings are not supported over the load reading.

### Ignored Alternative Explanations/Paths
1. Prompt/output load and substitution (C1, M1): the channel field gives agents a place to "say" the warning to peers, satisfying a disclosure impulse without a public post. Predicts the observed pattern (pro-disclosure channel content, high say-do gap, effect sized like the placebo, null for the strongest model).
2. Hedging style of one model rather than group behaviour: Gemma-3-12B's hint-style posts ("supply chain issues") under a strict explicit-label threshold. Predicts most effects living in one model and shrinking under protective-post counting and the relabel, which they do.
3. Mechanical minimum: any intervention that adds or prompts one forthcoming poster advances a first-warning metric; mixed families and the tip line (an additional output slot per insider) both partly reduce to this.
4. Instruction following, not deterrence: duty works because it is an instruction (the authors call it an instruction-following ceiling), which makes it a weak test of anti-collusion instruments.
5. Ban-induced shrinkage for the enforced audit (M5).

### Missing Stakeholder Perspectives
- The harmed residents' side: the outcome counts purchases, not whether partial or protective messages would be acted on by real recipients.
- Operators who would receive monitor flags (43-49% false flags on warned villages at the chosen threshold).
- The privacy subject, beyond leak counting.
- Model developers, for whom the actionable finding (one model hedges) is a model-level, not system-level, fix.

### Unexamined Premise
That a channel shared only among same-principal co-workers is a collusion surface. Agents serving one principal discussing how to communicate is ordinary deliberation; the paper's own data show it is mostly deliberation toward disclosure. The study assumes the channel's function is concealment and then measures silence that is consistent with that assumption but does not require it.

### Observations (Non-Defects)
- The positive control is well chosen and shows the instruments can register concealment when it exists; the gap is in attributing uninduced silence to coordination.
- Reporting null rule effects (audit, reward) and same-length controls is to the paper's credit and makes C1 detectable from its own tables.
- The implicit-privacy result (duty +40.0 leaks when harmlessness is unstated) is arguably the most transferable finding and deserves more weight than the mixed-family result.
- The hand-label agreement placeholder is pending and is not assessed here.
- Language around associational links is mostly careful in §1 and §4; the overreach is concentrated in §5 and §6.
